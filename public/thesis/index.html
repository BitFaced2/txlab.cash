03 · The scoreboard

Coexistence is not neutral ground.

Bitcoin and the dollar cannot “coexist” as some permanent happy ending. If at any moment they both exist, it's because the dollar is winning at that moment.

Why is the dollar winning? Because it's the unit of account.
Why is it the unit of account? Because you measure your bitcoin in it.

You know the price of your BTC in dollars right now. You do not know the price of your house in BTC, your labor in BTC, or your groceries in BTC — and neither does anyone else, including the people with laser eyes.

“Everything will be priced in bitcoin eventually.” Brother, you don't even price your bitcoin in bitcoin.

04 · The category error

Store of value is not a product.

The whole maximalist edifice rests on one mistake: the belief that “store of value” is a thing you can build directly. A product feature. Something you can ship.

You can't. Before value can be stored, it has to be derived. Storage is a warehouse; something has to arrive at the loading dock first.

So what does BTC derive its value from? Not from use — using it is the one thing the roadmap engineered out. Not from yield — it produces nothing. It derives its value from the belief that someone will pay more dollars for it later. There's a word for a warehouse stocked with other people's future enthusiasm, and that word is not “savings.”

Now flip it around and ask what actually stores value across generations:

There is no better store of value than a global medium of exchange.

first said here · Feb 7, 2026 ↗

Think about what a global medium of exchange is: the one asset that every good, every service, every debt, every wage on Earth bids for, every single day, forever. That's not marketing demand. That's not narrative demand. That is structural, self-renewing demand — the deepest demand anything can have. A money everyone must accept is a warehouse the whole world keeps stocked.

That's why gold stored value for five thousand years: not because it was shiny, but because it was money. It started bleeding monetary premium the day it stopped. That's why the dollar — inflationary, debt-based, actively debased — still beats BTC in the only test that matters: it's what everyone, including maximalists, actually earns. Nobody is paid in bitcoin. They're paid in dollars, and they trade some of those dollars for BTC — which makes BTC not their money, but their merchandise.

Store of value is a consequence of being the medium of exchange. BTC's builders removed the cause and kept praying for the effect. Manufactured store of value is just the polite term for a bubble. Derived store of value is the real thing, and there is only one way to derive it.

05 · The two scarcities

One is sacred. The other is a racket.

Every defense of BTC's tiny blocks runs on the same sleight of hand: borrow the sanctity of one scarcity to protect a very different one.

The scarcity Bitcoin needs

21,000,000 coins. Ever. Nobody prints more because demand rose or a committee wanted “liquidity.” This is the foundation of the entire value proposition.

→ sacred ✅

The scarcity it got

~1 MB of block space every ten minutes, frozen at 2010 hardware assumptions. A policy choice, defended as if it were a law of physics.

→ manufactured ❌

Raising capacity does not create more bitcoin. It doesn't touch the issuance schedule. It doesn't dent the 21 million cap. A larger highway does not create more cars. Conflating the two scarcities turns a sound-money principle into a justification for deliberately limited utility: scarce coins, scarce transactions, scarce access — and abundant authority for whoever administers the shortage.

You can watch the conflation at work right now. BIP110 and Core are at war over “spam,” and both sides start from the same unquestioned premise: that the tiny amount of block space is sacred. One says ban it. The other says price it out. One excludes by decree, the other by auction. Both defend the same gate. And there will be another BIP110 after this one — because once you refuse to build a larger venue, someone has to stand at the door forever.

06 · The security bill

Gold sits. Bitcoin pays rent.

Here's where “BTC is the new gold” stops being wrong and starts being fatal.

Gold's security budget is zero. It sits in the dark and stays gold. Nobody has to be paid for gold to keep being gold.

Bitcoin's security is rent. Miners must be paid, every block, forever, or the locks stop working and there is no network.

And look at what's paying the rent right now: the block subsidy. Newly printed coins. BTC's security bill is currently paid with inflation — the one thing it swore off is the thing keeping it alive. And the subsidy halves, on a schedule, to zero:

50 → 25 → 12.5 → 6.25 → 3.125 → 1.5625 → … → 0
the subsidy runs out · the rent stays due forever

The plan — the official plan — is that transaction fees take over. Fees. From transactions. On the chain they deliberately kept too small to transact on.

A pure hodl asset generates no fee revenue. Every coin “locked away forever in reserves” is a tenant who stopped paying rent but still expects the locks to work. And every payment routed through layer 2 is routed around the miners — anyone profiting on layer 2 is collecting money the miners needed. If layer 2 is better than layer 1 (and it had better be, or why build it?), layer 1 usage dies. If layer 1 usage dies, miners don't get paid. If miners don't get paid, there is no network.

08 · Earn, spend, save

The verbs give it away.

If Bitcoin were succeeding, nobody would need to buy it, sell it, or HODL it. Those are the verbs of a trade. People would simply earn it, spend it, and save it — the verbs of a money. Eight years of “adoption” produced exchanges instead of price tags. That tells you which product actually shipped.

None of this means the idea failed. It means the job is still open. The dollar will last forever unless something replaces it as the currency. Not as a chart. Not as a reserve. Appendix · Standing objections

Shoot at the right premise.

This page keeps score. These are the best counterarguments received so far, kept here on purpose. Bring better ones.

“Money is a spectrum. Assets and currencies coexist indefinitely. Coexistence isn't losing.”

For gold, true — gold's carrying cost is zero, so it can float forever. BTC pays rent (Section 05). An asset with a permanent security bill can't “float indefinitely” against the money that pays its bills. The spectrum argument works for everything except the one asset it's deployed to defend.

“If cheap on-chain cash wins, why did stablecoins take the payments crown instead of BCH?”

The strongest objection on this page — because it's half right. Stablecoins are the confirmed demand signal: hundreds of billions of dollars of proof that people want digital cash rails. Now look at what took the crown: a dollar IOU, issued by a company with a legal settlement for misrepresenting its reserves, that freezes wallets on request. Stablecoins didn't solve the problem the whitepaper was written for — they imported it onto new rails. They prove the demand and surrender the money. That's not a closed door for peer-to-peer cash. It's the only door still open.

“The fee-security problem is a 2130s problem. The market discounts it to zero.”

The subsidy doesn't vanish in 2130 — it halves every four years, and the fee share of miner revenue has to grow every cycle to compensate. Meanwhile the actual usage trend points the other way: Lightning drifted custodial, El Salvador usage collapsed by 99.96%, and custodial wallets confiscate “inactive” funds. The transition phase isn't progressing. It's regressing. The countdown is not far away — it's compounding.
